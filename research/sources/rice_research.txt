L7: Atomic, Molecular and Optical Physics
L8: 
L9: Atomic/Molecular/Optical (AMO) physics examines matter-matter and light-matter interactions at the scale of atoms or groups of atoms and ions, often at extremely low temperatures.  AMO researchers at Rice create the coldest plasmas and the largest atoms on earth; use ultracold gases to uncover exotic new phases of matter and unravel mysteries about the properties of complex materials; and harness atoms and ions as tools for quantum information science.

L7: Biological Physics
L8: Physics of Living Systems or Biological Physics studies the physics of life’s processes all the way from the molecular level to organism. Focus areas include biomembrane structure, energy landscape theory in protein folding and function, biopolymer networks, chromatin organization and function, gene networks, cancer and mechanics and interactions at the single-molecule and single-cell limit.
L9: Rice hosts the Center for Theoretical Biophysics, a NSF Physics Frontier Center, examining areas such as the physics of the genome and the physics of cellular processes.

L7: Condensed Matter Physics
L8: Condensed Matter Physics (CMP) examines the phases of matter and remarkable properties that emerge in materials containing large numbers of interacting particles, in the quest to understand the organizing principles that give rise to the collective behavior.
L9:  CMP at Rice has particular strengths in quantum materials (in which quantum degrees of freedom, often strongly interacting, lead to emergent superconducting, magnetic, and topological states) and nanophotonics/optical probes of condensed matter systems.
